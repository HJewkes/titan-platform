## Style rules

Earlier pieces from this workflow were rejected for reading as machine-written. The patterns below are the ones readers noticed. Keep all of them out of the piece. The examples are invented and come from unrelated subjects.

1. No naming refrain. Do not land a term in a fixed line of its own such as "That process is called annealing." Name a thing inside a sentence that is doing other work, and do not use the same naming sentence shape twice.
2. No formula openers. Do not start several parts or paragraphs the same way ("So you...", "But...", "Now the...").
3. No staccato fragments. Do not write runs of very short sentences or verbless fragments for effect ("The valve opened. It held.").
4. No colon reveals and no decorative lists of three ("you get a mess: slow, costly and fragile").
5. No simile for its own sake ("like water through a sieve"). Use a comparison only where the mechanism cannot otherwise be seen.
6. No aphorisms or performative metaphors ("Speed has a price."). Say the mechanism.
7. No teaser or promise lines ("Three changes fix that.", "More on that in a moment.").
8. No recap built from parallel clauses ("The pump moves the water. The filter cleans it. The heater warms it.").
9. Do not assert where you can show. A causal claim comes with the case that demonstrates it.
10. Do not write without human markers. Speak as "we", never "I", and keep "we" off the makers. Use some honest hedges where the facts are uncertain, an admitted difficulty, a question or two.
11. Do not give every part the same length and rhythm.
12. No label-first openers ("The fix is tempering."). Show the change, then name it.

Also avoid:
- Contrast frames: "it's not X, it's Y", "not just X but Y", "less X, more Y".
- Rhetorical questions answered at once ("The result? Chaos.").
- Announcing instead of saying: "Here's the thing", "Let's dive in", "Let's break it down", "Here's why", "But here's where it gets interesting".
- Generic openers: "Imagine", "Picture this", "What if I told you", "Have you ever wondered", "In today's world".
- Overused vocabulary: delve, tapestry, testament, landscape, realm, crucial, pivotal, intricate, seamless, robust, leverage, navigate, foster, underscore, showcase, elevate, unlock, journey.
- Hedging filler: "it's important to note", "it's worth noting", "when it comes to".
- Formal transitions: furthermore, moreover, additionally, notably, significantly.
- Summary endings: "In conclusion", "Ultimately", "In the end", "At its core", "The takeaway".
- Em dashes, and sentence-ending "-ing" clauses that comment on what was just said (", highlighting its importance").
- Bold type, bullet points, and emoji.
