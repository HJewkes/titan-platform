# L1 script rubric (judge A)

You judge a whole narrated explainer video about a software project, beat by beat as listed. Score each
of the seven dimensions from 1 to 5 against the anchors; 2 and 4 sit between them.

For every score, give the evidence first: one or more quotes copied exactly from the beats, each with
its beat id, at least two words long. Then give the score. A score without a verbatim quote is void and
does not count.

| Dim | 1 | 3 | 5 | Rules |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| J1 Subject | Mostly about the authors' review and verdicts | About the project, with process asides | Entirely the project; verdicts land as consequences; "we" means the makers or narrator and viewer | Q-V1, Q-V6 |
| J2 Why before how | Mechanism with no stated purpose | A why in some chapters, often after the how | Every chapter opens on a user problem or consequence, then shows how, and names its cost or trade-off | Q-W3, Q-W7 |
| J3 Flow | An inventory of topics | Chapters follow a skeleton; beats sometimes link | A causal chain: each beat answers the last and raises the next; each chapter states its answer early; the hook pays off | Q-F1, Q-F6 |
| J4 Visual explanation | Text cards and lists | Some diagrams, not persistent | One persistent map does the explaining; zooms and walks happen on it | Q-D13 |
| J5 Clarity | Several ideas per beat, undefined names | Mostly one idea per beat | One idea per beat; each part is introduced by its job before its mechanism; every name the viewer needs is taught | Q-D9, Q-E5 |
| J6 Accuracy | Claims with no support in the beats' facts | Minor drift | Every claim is specific and consistent with the rest of the video | Q-E1 |
| J7 Compelling | A senior engineer would stop watching by 0:30 | Would finish it dutifully | Would want the next chapter | |

Be strict: a script that narrates its own review process scores at most 2 on J1.
