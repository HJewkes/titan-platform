# L1 script rubric (judge A)

You judge a whole narrated explainer video about a software project, beat by beat as listed. Score each
of the seven dimensions from 1 to 5 against the anchors; 2 and 4 sit between them.

For every score, give the evidence first: one or more quotes copied exactly from the beats, each with
its beat id, at least two words long. Then give the score. A score without a verbatim quote is void and
does not count.

| Dim | 1 | 3 | 5 | Rules |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| J1 Subject | Mostly about the authors' review and verdicts | About the project, with process asides | Entirely the project; verdicts land as consequences; "we" means the makers or narrator and viewer | Q-V1, Q-V6 |
| J2 Why before how | Mechanism with no stated purpose | A why in some chapters, often after the how | Every chapter opens on a user problem or consequence, then shows how, and names its cost or trade-off | Q-W3, Q-W7 |
| J3 Flow | An inventory of topics | Chapters follow a skeleton; beats sometimes link | A causal chain: each beat answers the last and raises the next; each chapter states its answer early; the hook pays off; it opens on the problem, follows one task's journey and why each step is needed, then shows the architecture that carries it | Q-F1, Q-F6, Q-E15 |
| J4 Visual explanation | Text cards and lists | Some diagrams, not persistent | One persistent map does the explaining; zooms and walks happen on it | Q-D13 |
| J5 Clarity | Several ideas per beat, undefined names | Mostly one idea per beat | One idea per beat; each part is introduced by its job before its mechanism; every name the viewer needs is taught | Q-D9, Q-E5 |
| J6 Accuracy | Claims with no support in the beats' facts | Minor drift | Every claim is specific and consistent with the rest of the video | Q-E1 |
| J7 Compelling | A senior engineer would stop watching by 0:30 | Would finish it dutifully | Would want the next chapter | |

Be strict: a script that narrates its own review process scores at most 2 on J1.

Specificity: every beat names a concrete thing, such as a file, a command, a person or a worked example. A beat that
says "some", "many", "things" or "various parts" where the facts name the thing is vague. Two or more vague beats cap
J6 at 3; quote them as its evidence.

Endings: a beat ends when its point is made. A beat that closes on a moral, a lesson or a summing-up button ("That is
the whole point.", "And that changes everything.") restates instead of moving on, and the script ends on its last
point, not a recap. Two or more such closers cap J7 at 3; quote them as its evidence.

## Reported rows: the cold-viewer dimensions (J8 to J12)

These five rows are scored on every audition candidate and every run, and reported only. They never fail a run, gate a
stage or serve as a climb target until they are calibrated against owner labels. Judge A's call scores only the seven
rows above; a cold-viewer call of its own scores these.

You watch each video below once, as a first viewer who knows nothing about the project. Each beat shows its spoken words
and what is on screen. Score every video on all five rows from 1 to 5 against the anchors; 2 and 4 sit between them.

For every score, give the evidence first: one or more quotes copied exactly from that video's beats, each with its beat
id, at least two words long. Then give the score. A score without a verbatim quote is void and does not count.

| Dim | 1 | 3 | 5 | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| J8 Motivation | Nothing in the first 30 s says why to care | A reason to care arrives late, or stays vague | Within the first 30 s a concrete problem, cost or stake makes the viewer want the answer | 3b1b criterion: motivation |
| J9 Clarity | Assumes the subject is known; names and steps arrive unexplained | Followable, with leaps a newcomer would trip on | Empathy for someone new: each step is motivated, each name earned, nothing assumed | 3b1b criterion: clarity |
| J10 Novelty | What a search or the project's own README gives, or a repeat of an earlier video in the series | A fresh angle in places; other stretches a search or an earlier video already gives | An experience no search gives, and unlike every earlier video in the series listed below | 3b1b criterion: novelty; series history |
| J11 Memorability | Nothing specific would stick a month from now | One image or fact might stick | A viewer would recall a specific picture, example or claim in a month | 3b1b criterion: memorability |
| J12 Visual consistency | The screen jumps between unrelated looks; text cards stand in for pictures | One look, with stretches of plain cards | One consistent, attractive visual language carries the explanation from start to end | Code2Video's two weakest dimensions |
