A lighthouse keeper climbs the same stairs every night. The lamp at the top needs oil and a steady hand. Ships out on the water depend on that small flame. Without it, the rocks would claim another hull.

The keeper checks the lens before dusk falls. Salt spray clouds the glass over the day. A soft cloth wipes it clean again. The beam then reaches farther across the waves.

Fog is the real enemy on this coast. It rolls in from the sea without a sound. Light alone cannot cut through it. So the keeper rings the great bell instead.

The bell tolls once every thirty seconds. Captains learn to count the gaps between strokes. A long gap means open water ahead. A short one warns of the shore nearby.

Dawn finally washes the fog away. The keeper steps out onto the narrow gallery. Gulls wheel over the empty harbor. Another night has passed without a wreck.

Then the work starts over, as it always does. There is wood to split and rope to mend. The log book wants its daily entry too. By evening the stairs are waiting again.
