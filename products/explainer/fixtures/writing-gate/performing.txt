Imagine a kettle. Now imagine it never boiling. By the end of this, you'll be able to fix any kettle in your house.
Here's the thing nobody tells you about kettles. It isn't the water. It's the coil. The flat metal disc under the water is called the element. That is the heart of the kettle.
The element heats. The water waits. The steam builds. A thin strip of two metals bends when it gets hot. That is called the bimetallic strip. It clicks. It snaps. It decides everything.
Think of the strip like a tiny gatekeeper standing guard over your morning tea. The strip pushes a lever. That is called the trip switch. And that changes everything about how a kettle works.
Three fixes change that. Scale builds up on the element: hard, white and stubborn. The answer is descaling. Vinegar dissolves the scale, the scale leaves the element, and the element runs hot again.
Why does this matter? Because a kettle is more than a kettle. It is a promise of warmth. More on that later.
So remember. The element heats the water. The strip feels the heat. The switch cuts the power. The kettle rests. And that, really, is the whole story.
