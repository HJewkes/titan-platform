The script stage declares the heteronym readings a word's neighbours decide, so Q-V10 no longer stops a run on them. Every parsed draft or repair reply gets a `[w](=reading)` declaration only where a closed-class pattern allows one reading: "minutes" is the time unit, and so is "minute" after a counting word ("a", "one", "each", "more") when a pause or a function word follows; "minute" after "too", "quite", "rather", "extremely" or "most" is the adjective; a noun/verb heteronym after an article, possessive, "of" or "for" is the noun, and a base form after a mid-clause modal or negated "do", a base form after "I", "we" or "they", an -s form after "he", "she" or "it", and a base form after "to" with an object following are the verb; "read" after a perfect or passive auxiliary is past, and after a mid-clause modal or an infinitive "to" with an object is present. A pause before a word clears its left neighbour, so an earlier sentence never decides it, and a modal that opens its clause asks a question whose subject follows. An occurrence the rules leave open stays bare and still fails Q-V10. The l0 gate detail and the Q-V10 repair hint now name each undeclared word and its place in the line, numbers counted as words. A declared line now times, counts and captions as its plain words: the tts stage realigns the words a listener hears, so words.json and captions.vtt carry no `[w](=reading)` markup, and a cue may name a declared word; a beat opening on a declared word lands its uncued reveals on that word. The rewrite stage re-declares each parsed reply the same way, so a rewrite that drops a declaration its context decides is not reverted for Q-V10. Script stage version x16, rewrite x10, tts p6c.
